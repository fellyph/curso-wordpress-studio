# Video 01 script — Define the project with AI

**Estimated duration:** 5 minutes

## Hook · before slide 1

What does it cost to start building before you understand the problem? Usually, it costs rework, margin, and trust. That is why, before opening any building tool, our first task is to define clearly what we intend to deliver.

## Slide 1 · Before you build, define the project

Anyone leading an agency knows this situation: the most important part of a project often happens before the first line of code. It happens during the client conversation.

To make this concrete, I will use a case inspired by a real project I recently worked on: a website for an artificial intelligence technology event in Faro, in southern Portugal.

The event would have two tracks. In a meeting with the organizing community, we discussed its duration, expected attendance, location, and other important decisions. From that experience, I prepared a fictional conversation as the source material for our exercise.

In this first video, we are not choosing pages, components, or technology yet. First, we need to understand what should be built and why.

**Transition:** The starting point for that understanding is not a perfect prompt. It is a good conversation.

## Slide 2 · Start with the conversation

The simulated conversation represents a discovery meeting with the event organizers. It brings together the context of the AI event in Faro, the two-track proposal, and practical decisions such as timing, capacity, and location.

The goal is not to pretend that every answer is already available. It is to record what was said accurately and notice what still needs clarification.

For the workshop, we turned that conversation into fictional notes that are safe to share. Everyone can start from the same material and see how product decisions emerge from a meeting without exposing a real client's private information.

**Transition:** Once the conversation is recorded, AI becomes more than a text generator. It can help us interpret the project.

## Slide 3 · AI turns statements into decisions

How can artificial intelligence help here? A recording or transcript can be organized by participant, topic, and information type. AI can help separate who said what and classify the content.

From there, we can identify four important groups: the website's objective, the audiences it must serve, the requested functionality, and the questions that remain open.

We can also ask AI to distinguish confirmed facts, decisions, assumptions, and questions. For example: is the venue confirmed or still an option? Will registration be free? Who approves speaker selection? Which data truly needs to be collected?

These questions improve the context, but validation remains human. AI organizes the material and points out gaps; the team confirms what counts as a decision.

**Transition:** Once the conversation is clear, we can move through three phases without jumping straight into construction.

## Slide 4 · The conversation opens three phases

The first phase is discovery: listen, record, and clarify. This is where we gather context and ask questions until the important points are clear.

The second phase turns that understanding into requirements. We will record the purpose, audience, functionality, constraints, and open questions in a `requirements.md` file. That file will become the project's source of truth.

The third phase explores the design. With the requirements in hand, we can use a tool such as Claude Design or Google Stitch to produce the first drafts of the experience.

In my workflow, I chose Claude Design because I was already using Claude Max and it was the more convenient option for the work. The specific tool is not the essential part: you can use another solution that plays the same role.

Because the team was small, the first prototype helped make the proposal tangible quickly. Later, a designer joined the process and improved that prototype. AI does not have to remove human participation; it can provide a more concrete starting point for collaboration.

**Transition:** All of this works better when the conversation notes are reliable.

## Slide 5 · Good notes preserve what the conversation revealed

Good notes turn a conversation into shared memory. They preserve the context, decisions, questions, and constraints that must follow the project.

For agency teams, this also protects scope and profitability. The better we define the beginning, the less likely we are to discover too late that the client and team imagined different deliverables.

This care reduces rework and avoids unnecessary loops with AI. Instead of asking for undirected variations, we give the agent validated context and clear criteria.

Now we have the source material. In the next video, we will turn these notes into a `requirements.md`: a short, verifiable document ready to guide the design phase.

**Closing:** Before you build, define. Before asking AI for a solution, give it a well-understood problem.
